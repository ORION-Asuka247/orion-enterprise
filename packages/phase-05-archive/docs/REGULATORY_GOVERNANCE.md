# Regulatory Intelligence Governance

## Source hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Primary authority
Examples:
- enacted legislation
- statutory instruments
- authoritative legislation record

### Tier 2 — Official guidance
Examples:
- GOV.UK departmental guidance
- regulator guidance
- Approved Documents
- official regulator announcements

### Tier 3 — Professional guidance
Recognised professional and certification bodies.

### Tier 4 — Advisory
Trade articles, commentary and other secondary sources.

Tier 3 and Tier 4 sources must never independently create a statutory rule proposal.

## AI status

ORION AI is an analysis assistant, not the legal authority.

Every analysis must preserve:
- source URL
- document version
- retrieval time
- analysis model/version
- confidence
- citations
- human review decision

## Approval gate

A regulatory change cannot become approved unless the reviewer explicitly verifies:
- authoritative source
- applicability
- effective date

## Rule change gate

Approved regulatory change ≠ approved compliance rule.

A separate rule proposal must identify:
- existing rule/version
- proposed new logic
- evidence/source
- effective date
- affected template/questions
- rollback/supersession path

## Historical integrity

Changing a rule creates a new compliance rule version.

It never rewrites:
- old inspections
- old rule snapshots
- old reports
- old certificates

## Legal uncertainty

Where territorial or factual applicability is uncertain, ORION should display:
"Human specialist review required."

Do not manufacture certainty.
